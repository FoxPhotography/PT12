1. Gluteal Region (Top 3 Predicted 10-Mark Questions)
💡 ملخص سريع بالعامية — منطقة الإلية (Gluteal Region):

منطقة الإلية فيها ٣ أسئلة مقالي أساسية للميدتيرم: ١) عضلة Gluteus Maximus (أقوى Extensor لمفصل الحوض وبتتغذى بـ Inferior Gluteal N)، ٢) عضلة Gluteus Medius & Minimus وعلامة Trendelenburg (أهم مثبت للحوض أثناء المشي، لو انشل العصب Superior Gluteal يقع الحوض في الناحية العكسية)، ٣) الثقوب الوركية وحقن العضل الآمن (Piriformis هي المعلم، والحقن الآمن في Upper Outer Quadrant عشان نحمي العصب الوركي Sciatic N).

Q1
Describe the Gluteus Maximus Muscle (Origin, Insertion, Nerve Supply, Actions, Bursae & Clinical Testing).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Origin & Insertion (3 Marks)
  • Origin: Outer surface of ilium (behind posterior gluteal line), posterior surface of sacrum and coccyx, and sacrotuberous ligament.
  • Insertion:
    • Upper 3/4 (superficial fibers): Inserts into the Iliotibial tract (ITT).
    • Lower 1/4 (deep fibers): Inserts into the Gluteal tuberosity of the femur.
2. Nerve Supply (2 Marks)
  • Inferior Gluteal Nerve (L5, S1, S2) — enters the muscle on its deep surface.
3. Actions (3 Marks)
  • Chief powerful extensor of the hip joint (essential for rising from sitting, climbing stairs, and running; NOT used in normal flat walking).
  • Lateral rotator of the thigh at the hip joint.
  • Through the iliotibial tract, it stabilizes the extended knee joint.
4. Bursae & Clinical Testing (2 Marks)
  • Bursae: 1) Trochanteric bursa (between muscle and greater trochanter), 2) Ischial bursa (over ischial tuberosity — "Weaver's bottom").
  • Clinical Test: Patient lies prone and raises the thigh against resistance while the examiner palpates muscle contraction.
Q2
Describe Gluteus Medius & Minimus Muscles, and explain the mechanism and causes of a Positive Trendelenburg Sign and Gait.
10 MARKS
▼
1. Origin, Insertion & Nerve Supply (3 Marks)
  • Origin: Outer surface of ilium (Medius: between anterior & posterior gluteal lines; Minimus: between anterior & inferior lines).
  • Insertion: Greater trochanter of femur (Medius: lateral surface; Minimus: anterior border).
  • Nerve Supply: Superior Gluteal Nerve (L4, L5, S1).
2. Main Actions (2 Marks)
  • Chief abductors of the hip joint.
  • Anterior fibers cause medial rotation of the thigh.
  • Pelvic Stabilizer: During single-leg stance, they contract to keep the pelvis level, preventing the unsupported side from sagging.
3. Trendelenburg Sign: Mechanism & Interpretation (3 Marks)
  • Normal: When standing on the right leg, right abductors contract → left side of pelvis rises slightly.
  • Positive Trendelenburg: When standing on the affected (stance) leg, the pelvis DROPS on the opposite (unsupported swing) side.
  • Conclusion: A positive sign on the left side of the pelvis indicates weakness of the RIGHT abductors / Right Superior Gluteal Nerve.
4. Causes & Gait (2 Marks)
  • Causes: 1) Superior gluteal nerve injury, 2) Polio / muscular dystrophy, 3) Congenital dislocation of the hip (CDH), 4) Coxa vara / fracture neck of femur.
  • Gait: Unilateral lesion causes Lurching Gait (trunk leans toward the affected supported side); bilateral lesion causes Waddling (Duck-like) Gait.
Q3
Enumerate the structures passing through the Greater and Lesser Sciatic Foramina, and explain the safe site for Intramuscular (IM) Injection in the Gluteal Region.
10 MARKS
▼
1. Key Landmark Muscle (1 Mark)
  • Piriformis Muscle: Emerges through the Greater Sciatic Foramen and divides it into structures above and below it.
2. Structures Passing Through Greater Sciatic Foramen (4 Marks)
  • Above Piriformis: Superior Gluteal Nerve and Superior Gluteal Vessels (Artery & Vein).
  • Below Piriformis:
    • Sciatic Nerve (largest nerve in the body).
    • Inferior Gluteal Nerve and Inferior Gluteal Vessels.
    • Posterior Cutaneous Nerve of Thigh.
    • Nerve to Quadratus Femoris.
    • PIN structures: Pudendal nerve, Internal pudendal vessels, Nerve to obturator internus.
3. Structures Passing Through Lesser Sciatic Foramen (2 Marks)
  • Tendon of Obturator Internus muscle.
  • PIN structures (re-entering perineum): Pudendal Nerve, Internal Pudendal Vessels, Nerve to Obturator Internus.
4. Safe Site for IM Gluteal Injection (3 Marks)
  • The buttock is divided into 4 quadrants.
  • ONLY Safe Quadrant: Upper Outer (Anterosuperior) Quadrant into the bulky Gluteus Medius muscle.
  • Anatomical Reason: The Sciatic Nerve curves through the lower and medial quadrants, and superior gluteal vessels lie medially. Injection in any other quadrant risks piercing the sciatic nerve, causing Foot Drop and paralysis.

2. Femoral Triangle & Canal (Top 2 Predicted 10-Mark Questions)
💡 ملخص سريع بالعامية — مثلث الفخذ والقناة الفخذية:

مثلث الفخذ: حدوده (أعلى Inguinal Ligament، برة Sartorius، جوة Adductor Longus). محتوياته من برة لجوة NAVL (عصب، شريان، وريد، ليمف). الغمد الفخذي: العصب الفخذي برة الغمد تماماً! والقناة الفخذية (Femoral Canal) هي المسكن الإنسي، والفتق الفخذي فيها بيختنق بسرعة عشان حدوده الحادة الصلبة خصوصاً Lacunar Ligament.

Q4
Describe the Femoral Triangle in detail (Boundaries, Roof, Floor, Contents with relations from lateral to medial, and Clinical Anatomy).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Location & Boundaries (3 Marks)
  • Subfascial triangular space in the upper 1/3 of the anterior thigh.
  • Base (Superior): Inguinal Ligament (extends from ASIS to Pubic Tubercle).
  • Lateral Boundary: Medial border of Sartorius muscle.
  • Medial Boundary: Medial border of Adductor Longus muscle.
  • Apex (Inferior): Point where Sartorius crosses Adductor Longus (continuous with Adductor Canal).
2. Floor & Roof (2 Marks)
  • Floor (Gutter-shaped, lateral to medial): 1) Iliopsoas, 2) Pectineus, 3) Adductor Longus.
  • Roof: Skin, superficial fascia (contains Great Saphenous Vein, superficial arteries, femoral branch of genitofemoral nerve), and Fascia Lata with Cribriform Fascia covering the Saphenous Opening.
3. Contents from Lateral to Medial (NAVL) (3 Marks)
  • 1. Femoral Nerve (N): Lies laterally outside the femoral sheath in the groove between Psoas and Iliacus; terminates into anterior and posterior divisions.
  • 2. Femoral Artery (A): Main artery, gives off Profunda Femoris Artery (deep artery of thigh).
  • 3. Femoral Vein (V): Receives the Great Saphenous Vein and Profunda Femoris Vein.
  • 4. Deep Inguinal Lymphatics / Canal (L): Deep lymph nodes (Node of Cloquet).
4. Clinical Applied Anatomy (2 Marks)
  • Femoral Pulse Palpation: Felt at the Mid-Inguinal Point (midway between ASIS and Pubic Symphysis).
  • Femoral Artery Cannulation: Used for left heart catheterization / coronary angiography.
Q5
Describe the Femoral Sheath, Femoral Canal & Ring (Boundaries, Contents, and compare Femoral vs Inguinal Hernia).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Femoral Sheath: Formation & 3 Compartments (3 Marks)
  • Funnel-shaped fascial prolongation (about 3.75 cm long) surrounding the femoral vessels.
  • Formation: Anterior wall from Fascia Transversalis; Posterior wall from Fascia Iliaca.
  • 3 Compartments:
    • Lateral compartment: Contains Femoral Artery and Femoral branch of Genitofemoral Nerve.
    • Intermediate compartment: Contains Femoral Vein.
    • Medial compartment (Femoral Canal): Contains lymphatics and Node of Cloquet.
    • NOTE: Femoral Nerve is OUTSIDE the sheath.
2. Femoral Canal & Femoral Ring Boundaries (3 Marks)
  • Femoral Canal: Conical dead space (1.25 cm long); allows expansion of the femoral vein during increased venous return.
  • Femoral Ring (Upper abdominal opening of canal) Boundaries:
    • Anterior: Inguinal Ligament.
    • Posterior: Pectineus muscle and Pectineal Ligament of Cooper.
    • Medial: Sharp crescentic free edge of Lacunar Ligament (Gimbernat's).
    • Lateral: Femoral Vein.
3. Femoral Hernia vs Inguinal Hernia Comparison (4 Marks)
Feature Femoral Hernia Inguinal Hernia
Neck / Position Below & Lateral to Pubic Tubercle Above & Medial to Pubic Tubercle
Sex Predilection More common in Females (wider pelvis & larger canal) More common in Males
Strangulation Risk High Risk (Early Strangulation) due to narrow, rigid neck & sharp lacunar ligament Lower risk compared to femoral hernia
Surgical Danger Abnormal obturator artery (crosses lacunar ligament in 20% of cases) Inferior epigastric vessels

3. Anterior & Medial Thigh (Top 2 Predicted 10-Mark Questions)
💡 ملخص سريع بالعامية — عضلات الفخذ الأمامية والإنسية:

عضلة الرباعية (Quadriceps): أقوى باسط للركبة، وتغذيها الفروع الخلفية للعصب الفخذي، وRectus femoris الوحيدة اللي بتبسط الركبة وتثني الحوض. قناة الصياد (Hunter's Canal): نفق تحت عضلة Sartorius بين المسكن الأمامي والإنسي بينتهي بـ Adductor Hiatus. العضلة الهجينة Adductor Magnus: بتشتغل مقربة وباسطة للحوض وبتاخد عصبين (Obturator + Sciatic). قدم الإوزة (Pes Anserinus): تجميعة ٣ عضلات من ٣ مساكن و٣ أعصاب مختلفة (SGS).

Q6
Describe the Adductor Canal (Hunter's / Subsartorial Canal) in detail (Extent, Boundaries, Contents, Structures entering vs leaving, and Clinical Significance).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Definition & Extent (2 Marks)
  • Intermuscular aponeurotic tunnel (about 15 cm long) in the middle 1/3 of the medial side of the thigh.
  • Extent: From the apex of the Femoral Triangle above to the Adductor Hiatus in the Adductor Magnus muscle below.
2. Boundaries (3 Marks)
  • Anteromedial (Roof): Sartorius muscle and subsartorial fascial plexus of nerves.
  • Anterolateral: Vastus Medialis muscle.
  • Posterior (Floor): Adductor Longus (above) and Adductor Magnus (below).
3. Contents & Piercing Structures (3 Marks)
  • 1. Femoral Artery: Traverses the entire length and enters the popliteal fossa through Adductor Hiatus as Popliteal Artery.
  • 2. Femoral Vein: Lies posterior to artery above, then posterolateral below.
  • 3. Saphenous Nerve: Crosses anterior to the femoral artery from lateral to medial, then pierces the roof to become cutaneous.
  • 4. Nerve to Vastus Medialis: Enters the muscle in the upper part of the canal.
  • 5. Descending Genicular Artery: Arises near the lower end to contribute to anastomoses around the knee.
4. Clinical Importance (2 Marks)
  • Adductor Canal Block: Local anesthetic injected around the saphenous nerve for postoperative knee pain management (spares motor quadriceps strength).
  • Hunter's Ligation: Historical ligation of femoral artery in the canal for popliteal aneurysm.
Q7
Explain why Adductor Magnus is a composite (hybrid) muscle, and describe the anatomy of Pes Anserinus (SGS) and its clinical importance.
10 MARKS
▼
1. Adductor Magnus as a Composite / Hybrid Muscle (5 Marks)
Feature Adductor Part (Pubic fibers) Hamstring Part (Ischial fibers)
Origin Ischiopubic ramus Ischial tuberosity
Insertion Linea aspera & medial supracondylar line Adductor Tubercle of femur
Nerve Supply Obturator Nerve (L2, L3, L4 - Post. division) Tibial part of Sciatic Nerve (L4)
Action Adducts and flexes the thigh at hip Extends the thigh at hip (like hamstrings)
• Adductor Hiatus: Gap between the two insertions through which the femoral vessels pass into the popliteal fossa.
2. Anatomy of Pes Anserinus (SGS) (3 Marks)
  • Conjoined tendon insertion of 3 muscles from 3 different compartments into the upper medial surface of the tibia:
    • Sartorius: Anterior compartment → Femoral Nerve.
    • Gracilis: Medial compartment → Obturator Nerve.
    • Semitendinosus: Posterior compartment → Sciatic Nerve (Tibial part).
  • Mnemonic: SGS (Say Grace before Supper).
3. Clinical Applied Anatomy (2 Marks)
  • Pes Anserine Bursitis: Inflammation of the bursa deep to the tendons; causes medial knee pain and tenderness in runners and osteoarthritis patients.
  • Graft Harvest: Gracilis and Semitendinosus tendons are commonly harvested for ACL reconstruction surgery.

4. Hamstrings & Popliteal Fossa (Top 2 Predicted 10-Mark Questions)
💡 ملخص سريع بالعامية — عضلات الهامسترينج والحفرة المأبضية:

عضلات الهامسترينج: ليها ٤ شروط ثابتة (أصلها Ischial tuberosity، اندغامها في عظام الساق، بتعدي على مفصلين، وعصبها Tibial). الرأس القصيرة للبايسبس مستثناة لأن عصبها Common Fibular ومش ماسكة في الإسكيم. الحفرة المأبضية (Popliteal Fossa): معين ورا الركبة، ترتيب محتوياته من السطح للعمق (عصب قصبي ثم وريد مأبضي ثم شريان مأبضي). عضلة Popliteus: مفتاح فك قفل الركبة (Unlocking).

Q8
Describe the Hamstring Muscles (4 Criteria of a True Hamstring, OINA table, and why Short Head of Biceps is not a true hamstring).
10 MARKS
▼
1. The 4 Criteria of a "True Hamstring" (3 Marks)
  • 1) Must arise from the Ischial Tuberosity.
  • 2) Must insert into the bones of the leg (Tibia or Fibula).
  • 3) Must cross two joints (Hip & Knee joints).
  • 4) Must be supplied by the Tibial component of the Sciatic Nerve.
2. Hamstring Muscles OINA Summary Table (4 Marks)
Muscle Origin Insertion Nerve & Action
Semimembranosus Upper outer ischial tuberosity Groove on medial condyle of tibia Tibial N. (L5, S1, S2) → Flexes knee, extends hip
Semitendinosus Lower inner ischial tuberosity Upper medial tibia (Pes anserinus) Tibial N. (L5, S1, S2) → Flexes knee, extends hip
Biceps Femoris (Long Head) Lower inner ischial tuberosity Head of Fibula Tibial N. (L5, S1, S2) → Flexes knee, lateral rotator
Ischial part of Adductor Magnus Ischial tuberosity Adductor tubercle of femur Tibial N. (L4) → Extends hip joint
3. Why is Short Head of Biceps Femoris NOT a True Hamstring? (3 Marks)
  • 1. Origin: Arises from the linea aspera of femur (NOT ischial tuberosity).
  • 2. Joints crossed: Crosses only ONE joint (the knee joint, not the hip).
  • 3. Nerve Supply: Supplied by the Common Fibular (Peroneal) Nerve (NOT the tibial nerve).
Q9
Describe the Popliteal Fossa in detail (Boundaries, Roof, Floor, Contents from superficial to deep, and Clinical Anatomy: Popliteus unlocking role & Baker's cyst).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Boundaries (3 Marks)
  • Diamond-shaped intermuscular space behind the knee joint.
  • Superomedial: Semimembranosus and Semitendinosus.
  • Superolateral: Biceps Femoris.
  • Inferomedial: Medial head of Gastrocnemius.
  • Inferolateral: Lateral head of Gastrocnemius and Plantaris.
2. Roof & Floor (2 Marks)
  • Roof: Skin, superficial fascia (contains Small Saphenous Vein and Posterior Cutaneous Nerve of Thigh), and deep popliteal fascia.
  • Floor (Superior to Inferior): 1) Popliteal surface of femur, 2) Oblique popliteal ligament of knee joint, 3) Popliteus fascia over Popliteus muscle.
3. Contents from Superficial to Deep (3 Marks)
  • 1. Tibial Nerve: Most superficial central structure; bisects the fossa vertically.
  • 2. Common Fibular Nerve: Follows the medial border of the biceps femoris tendon superolaterally.
  • 3. Popliteal Vein: Lies deep to tibial nerve, between nerve and artery.
  • 4. Popliteal Artery: Deepest structure, lying directly against the floor and joint capsule.
4. Clinical Applied Anatomy (2 Marks)
  • Popliteus Muscle ("Key to Knee"): Unlocks the locked knee joint at the beginning of flexion by laterally rotating the femur on fixed tibia.
  • Baker's Cyst (Popliteal Cyst): Swelling caused by distension of the semimembranosus/gastrocnemius bursa; common in rheumatoid arthritis and knee osteoarthritis.
  • Popliteal Pulse: Palpated deep in the midline of the fossa with the knee flexed to relax the popliteal fascia.

5. Nerves of the Lower Limb (Top 4 Predicted 10-Mark Questions)
💡 ملخص سريع بالعامية — أعصاب الطرف السفلي:

العصب الفخذي (Femoral N - L2,L3,L4): أكبر عصب في الضفيرة القطنية، مسؤل عن بسط الركبة ومنعكس Knee Jerk. العصب السدادي (Obturator N - L2,L3,L4): بيغذي المقربات وقانون هيلتون بيفسر وجع الركبة في أمراض مفصل الحوض. العصب الوركي (Sciatic N - L4-S3): أثخن عصب في الجسم. العصب الشظوي (Common Fibular): بيلف حول عنق الشظية وإصابته بتعمل سقوط قدم Foot Drop ومشية High-Stepping Gait.

Q10
Describe the Femoral Nerve (Origin & Root value, Course, Branches, Patellar Reflex, and Effects of Nerve Injury).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Origin, Root Value & Course (3 Marks)
  • Origin: Largest branch of the Lumbar Plexus; arises from the Posterior divisions of anterior rami of L2, L3, L4.
  • Course: Emerges from the lateral border of Psoas Major, descends in the groove between Psoas and Iliacus, passes behind the Inguinal Ligament OUTSIDE the femoral sheath into the Femoral Triangle.
  • Termination: About 2.5 cm below inguinal ligament, it divides into Anterior and Posterior divisions.
2. Branches (3 Marks)
  • Anterior Division:
    • Muscular: Nerve to Sartorius.
    • Cutaneous: Medial & Intermediate Cutaneous Nerves of Thigh.
  • Posterior Division:
    • Muscular: Nerves to 4 heads of Quadriceps Femoris (Rectus Femoris, Vastus Medialis, Lateralis, Intermedius) and Articularis Genu.
    • Cutaneous: Saphenous Nerve (longest cutaneous nerve; supplies medial leg and foot up to the base of the great toe).
3. Clinical Anatomy & Injury Effects (4 Marks)
  • Motor Loss: Complete paralysis of Quadriceps Femoris → Loss of knee extension; patient cannot stand steadily or climb stairs without supporting the knee by hand.
  • Sensory Loss: Loss of sensation on the anterior and medial thigh, and medial aspect of the leg and foot (Saphenous distribution).
  • Reflex Loss: Loss of Knee Jerk (Patellar Reflex - L3, L4).
Q11
Describe the Obturator Nerve (Origin, Course, Divisions around Adductor Brevis, Motor/Sensory Distribution, and Hilton's Law).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Origin & Course (3 Marks)
  • Origin: Arises from the Anterior divisions of anterior rami of L2, L3, L4 of the Lumbar Plexus.
  • Course: Descends through Psoas Major, crosses the pelvic brim, runs along the lateral pelvic wall, and enters the thigh through the Obturator Canal.
  • Division: Divides into Anterior and Posterior divisions as it straddles the Adductor Brevis muscle.
2. Distribution of Anterior & Posterior Divisions (4 Marks)
  • Anterior Division (Passes in front of Adductor Brevis):
    • Muscular: Adductor Longus, Adductor Brevis, Gracilis, and Pectineus (occasionally).
    • Cutaneous: Small patch on the medial side of the middle 1/3 of thigh.
    • Articular: Hip joint.
  • Posterior Division (Passes behind Adductor Brevis):
    • Muscular: Obturator Externus, Adductor part of Adductor Magnus.
    • Articular: Knee joint (pierces oblique popliteal ligament).
3. Hilton's Law & Clinical Significance (3 Marks)
  • Hilton's Law: The nerve that supplies muscles moving a joint also supplies the joint capsule and the skin over the muscle insertions.
  • Referred Pain: Because the Obturator Nerve supplies both the hip joint and knee joint, disease of the hip (e.g., tuberculosis, Perthes disease, arthritis) frequently presents as referred pain in the knee in children.
Q12
Describe the Sciatic Nerve (Origin & Roots, Course in Gluteal Region & Thigh, Relations, Piriformis Syndrome, and Sciatica with SLR Test).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Origin & Roots (2 Marks)
  • Thickest nerve in the body (about 2 cm wide).
  • Root Value: L4, L5, S1, S2, S3 (Sacral Plexus). Composed of two nerves in a common sheath: Tibial Nerve (anterior divisions L4-S3) and Common Fibular Nerve (posterior divisions L4-S2).
2. Course & Relations (3 Marks)
  • Gluteal Region: Enters via Greater Sciatic Foramen below Piriformis; descends midway between Greater Trochanter and Ischial Tuberosity under cover of Gluteus Maximus.
  • Thigh: Runs down the posterior compartment on Adductor Magnus, crossed obliquely by Long Head of Biceps Femoris.
  • Termination: Bifurcates into Tibial Nerve and Common Fibular Nerve at the upper angle of the Popliteal Fossa.
3. Branches (2 Marks)
  • Muscular branches to all Hamstrings (Semimembranosus, Semitendinosus, Biceps Femoris) and hamstring part of Adductor Magnus.
  • Articular branches to hip and knee joints.
4. Clinical Anatomy (3 Marks)
  • Sciatica: Radiating pain along the sciatic distribution (buttock, back of thigh, posterolateral leg) commonly caused by herniated lumbar disc (L4/L5 or L5/S1).
  • Straight Leg Raise (SLR / Lasegue's) Test: Passive elevation of the extended leg stretches the sciatic root, producing sharp pain between 30° and 70°.
  • Piriformis Syndrome: Spasm or hypertrophy of the Piriformis muscle compressing the sciatic nerve.
Q13
Compare Common Fibular (Peroneal) Nerve Injury vs Tibial Nerve Injury (Vulnerability, Deformities, Motor Loss, Sensory Loss, and Gaits).
10 MARKS
▼
1. Detailed Comparison Table (7 Marks)
Feature Common Fibular Nerve Lesion Tibial Nerve Lesion
Site of Vulnerability Subcutaneous winding around the Neck of the Fibula (fractures, tight plaster casts) Deep in Popliteal Fossa or under Flexor Retinaculum behind Medial Malleolus (Tarsal Tunnel)
Characteristic Deformity Foot Drop (Equinovarus): Plantarflexed & Inverted foot Calcaneovalgus: Dorsiflexed & Everted foot
Motor Deficit Loss of Dorsiflexion (Deep fibular) and Eversion (Superficial fibular) Loss of Plantarflexion (Gastrocnemius, Soleus) & Inversion (Tibialis posterior)
Functional Inability Cannot walk on Heels Cannot stand on Tiptoes
Sensory Loss Anterolateral leg, dorsum of foot, and 1st web space Sole of the foot (trophic ulcers risk) and posterolateral lower leg
Resulting Gait High-Stepping Gait (Cock-Walk): Knee raised high to avoid dragging toes Shuffling Gait: Loss of forward push-off thrust
Reflex Affected None Loss of Ankle Jerk (Achilles Reflex - S1, S2)
2. Deep Fibular vs Superficial Fibular Nerve Summary (3 Marks)
  • Deep Fibular Nerve: Supplies anterior compartment muscles (Tibialis Anterior, EHL, EDL, Peroneus Tertius) + Extensor Digitorum Brevis. Sensory: 1st interdigital web space ONLY.
  • Superficial Fibular Nerve: Supplies lateral compartment muscles (Peroneus Longus & Brevis). Sensory: Lower anterolateral leg and most of the dorsum of foot.

📌 Midterm Quick Sheet
Nerve Roots Summary
  • Femoral Nerve: L2, L3, L4 (Post. divisions)
  • Obturator Nerve: L2, L3, L4 (Ant. divisions)
  • Superior Gluteal: L4, L5, S1
  • Inferior Gluteal: L5, S1, S2
  • Sciatic Nerve: L4, L5, S1, S2, S3
  • Common Fibular: L4, L5, S1, S2 (Neck of fibula)
  • Tibial Nerve: L4, L5, S1, S2, S3
Clinical Deformities
  • Foot Drop: Common Fibular N. lesion
  • Calcaneovalgus: Tibial N. lesion
  • Trendelenburg: Superior Gluteal N. lesion (Stance side)
  • Knee Jerk Lost: Femoral N. (L3, L4)
  • Ankle Jerk Lost: Tibial N. (S1, S2)
Must-Remember Mnemonics
  • NAVL (Femoral Triangle): Nerve, Artery, Vein, Lymph (Lat to Med)
  • SGS (Pes Anserinus): Sartorius, Gracilis, Semitendinosus
  • PIN (GSF to LSF): Pudendal N, Int. Pudendal, N to Obturator Int
