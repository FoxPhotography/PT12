Ultraviolet Radiation 
(UVR)
 Ultraviolet radiation is electromagnetic 
energy, which is invisible to the human 
eye, with wavelengths between 100 nm 
to 400 nm. Divided into three ranges: 
 UV-A = Near UV (315nm-400nm)
 Little or no physiologic effect
 UV-B = Middle UV (280nm-315nm)
 Sunburn and age-related skin 
changes
 UV-C = Far UV (100nm-280nm)
 Bactericidal

 Unlike X-rays, ultraviolet radiation has a 
low power of penetration; hence, its 
direct effects on the human body are 
limited to the surface skin. The direct

Why X
-
rays 
penetrate deeper 
than ultraviolet 
(UV) light?

=== PAGE 14 ===
primarily due to their higher energy and shorter 
wavelength. Here’s a more detailed explanation
Energy Levels: X-rays have much higher energy 
photons compared to UV light. This high energy allows 
X-rays to interact with matter at a deeper level, 
enabling them to break atomic bonds and pass 
through dense materials, including human tissues and 
various substances.
Wavelength: X-rays have shorter wavelengths than UV 
light. The shorter wavelength allows X-rays to interact 
less with the electrons in atoms. This means they can 
pass through materials more easily, as they are less 
likely to be absorbed or scattered.

=== PAGE 15 ===
Cont.
In summary, the combination of higher energy and shorter wavelength allows X-
rays to penetrate deeper than ultraviolet light.
Interaction with Matter: When light interacts with matter, it can be absorbed or 
scattered. UV light tends to be absorbed by electrons in atoms, especially in 
organic materials. X-rays, on the other hand, can penetrate deeper without being 
significantly absorbed, which is why they are used in medical imaging.

=== PAGE 25 ===
1. Type of Radiation:
1. Ultraviolet Light: UV radiation primarily affects the skin's 
outer layers (epidermis) and is known to cause damage to 
DNA in the cells. This damage leads to inflammation and 
erythema (redness) as a direct result of the biological 
reaction to this damage.
2. Infrared Light: Infrared radiation penetrates deeper into the 
skin and primarily causes heating. It does not cause direct 
DNA damage like UV but instead raises the temperature of 
the tissue, leading to a uniform increase in blood flow.

=== PAGE 26 ===
2. Skin Response:
1. Erythema (UV exposure): When the skin is exposed to UV 
light, especially UVB radiation, it induces a localized 
inflammatory response, resulting in patchy redness and 
swelling. This occurs because the immune response is 
activated due to cellular damage, which can lead to 
uneven or mottled appearances if the exposure varies 
across the skin.
2. Uniform Heating (IR exposure): Infrared light warms up 
the tissue uniformly, which usually leads to a consistent 
and even erythema if any redness is produced. The heating 
can increase blood flow uniformly across the area exposed, 
thereby contributing to a more homogeneous appearance.


=== PAGE 27 ===
3. Mechanism of Action:
• Mottled Appearance (from UV): The mottled appearance can 
be due to varying degrees of individual skin response or uneven 
exposure to UV radiation (like in sunburn), where some areas 
may be more sensitive or were exposed longer than others.
• Uniform Appearance (from IR): Infrared exposure tends to be 
more evenly distributed across the surface area, leading to a 
more uniform appearance in terms of warmth and potential 
redness but not the same inflammatory response as with UV .

2) Pigmentation :
• Pigmentation of the skin occurs as a 
result of both the formation of melanin in 
the deep region of the epidermis and the 
migration of melanin already formed into 
more superficial layers.
• In already pigmented individuals some 
immediate tanning may occur within as 
little as 10 minutes of exposure, due to 
darkening of existing melanin by photo-
oxidation.

Growth
• Stimulation by ultraviolet radiation 
provokes increased keratinocyte cell 
turnover so that the skin grows more

cells at an earlier stage in their 
development than usual so that they 
remain in pieces, or even sheets, and 
can be peeled off.
