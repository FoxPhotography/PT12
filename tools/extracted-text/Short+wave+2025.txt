=== PAGE 34 ===
• SWD applicators differ in how they deliver energy—and thus where that 
energy concentrates—based on tissue conductivity (σ) and impedance 
(Z).
1.Capacitive (Plate‐to‐Plate) Application
1.Mechanism: Two opposing metal plates create an oscillating electric 
field. Current flows from one plate, through the patient’s tissues, to 
the other plate.
2.Heat Generation:
• P = I²·R (Joule’s law). Low-conductivity tissues (i.e., adipose, high R) 
force higher voltage drops → greater local heating.
• At interfaces (e.g., bone/muscle), abrupt changes in σ cause 
concentration of the electric field → “hot spots.”
3.Depth Profile:
• Most energy is absorbed in the first few centimeters, especially in 
fat layers and at tissue boundaries.

=== PAGE 35 ===
• Induction (Drum or Coil) Application
• Mechanism: A coil produces a time‐varying magnetic field that penetrates 
tissues unimpeded. This induces circular eddy currents in conductive tissues.
• Heat Generation:
• Eddy currents preferentially flow where σ is highest—namely muscle (high 
water/ion content).
• Lower resistance means more current but less voltage drop; because of the 
large current loops, total heating remains greatest in muscle.
• Depth Profile:
• Magnetic fields are not shunted by superficial fat; they penetrate more 
uniformly, focusing heating in deeper, well-perfused musculature.

=== PAGE 38 ===
• The electrical conductivity  of tissue depends 
primarily on 
•    1- Tissue type.                                       2- Frequency of the 
signal being applied.
• Metals and tissues with a high water and electrolyte 
content, such as muscle or synovial fluid, have high 
electrical conductivity, whereas tissues with low water 
content, such as fat, bone, and collagen, have low 
electrical conductivity.
• Thus, inductive coils can heat both deep and superficial 
tissues, but they produce the most heat in tissues with the 
highest electrical conductivity.

=== PAGE 39 ===
• the dielectric characteristics of tissues can also 
cause different temperature variations in them, since 
they offer variable resistance to the current passage. 
Thus, tissues like the adipose offer a high resistance 
to the current passage, and present a superior 
heating when compared to the muscular tissue, 
which due to its greater amount of electrolytes, 
presents a lower resistance to the radiofrequency 
currents.
