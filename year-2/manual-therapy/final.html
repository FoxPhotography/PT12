Detail the role of Ruffini endings in slow sustained Myofascial Release (MFR).
▼
Ruffini Endings Mechanism

Ruffini corpuscles are specialized mechanoreceptors that play a central role in myofascial release:

  • Slow-Adapting Receptors: They continue firing nerve impulses to the brain as long as lateral stretch is sustained, unlike fast-adapting receptors that fire only when a change occurs.
  • Lateral Stretch Response: They are stimulated by manual pressure applied in the same plane as the underlying fascia (cross-hand or sustained glide).
  • Central Integration (Schleip Theory): The brain processes the continuous sensory input from Ruffini endings. If evaluated as safe, the brain responds by reducing motor neuron output, leading to:
    • Decreased local muscle tone.
    • Dilation of blood vessels (increasing circulation).
    • Systemic relaxation and anxiety reduction.

▼
Differential Criteria
  1. Overloading: History of acute, chronic, or persistent muscle overload.
  2. Pain Referral: Distinct referred pain pattern inherent to that specific muscle.
  3. Reduced Performance: Diminished strength and limited lengthening capacity of the muscle.
  4. Palpable Band: Palpable taut band or nodule within the muscle belly.
  5. Tenderness: Extreme tenderness of the palpable nodule on compression.
  6. Twitch Response: Elicitation of a local twitch response when the taut band is snapped transversely.
  7. Pain Replication: Replication of the patient's familiar pain complaint when the point is compressed.
  8. Relief: Asymptomatic relief (reduced pain and increased ROM) following successful intervention.

▼
TrP Phases
  • Phase 1 (Active Constant): Palpable nodular mass in muscle, fascia, or ligament. Replicates referred pain constantly, both at rest and during activity.

Explain the Neurophysiological Mechanism of Acupuncture Pain Management (Kendall 1980).
▼
Kendall Mechanism

According to Kendall (1980), needle insertion at an acupoint sets off a cascade of neurological events:

  1. Inflammatory Response: Needle insertion causes microscopic local tissue injury, triggering a localized inflammatory response.
  2. Dorsal Horn Entry: Pain signals travel along small myelinated A-delta fibers to the dorsal horn of the spinal cord.
  3. Gamma Loop & Crossover: Signals activate crossover neurons and the gamma loop, modulating spindle sensitivity.
  4. Somatic Motor Activation: Stimulates motor nerves, reducing muscle spasm.
  5. Autonomic Regulation: Autonomic nerves are stimulated to alter blood flow and promote healing.
  6. Chemical Release: The brain releases endogenous opioids (endorphins, enkephalins) and suppresses Substance P, leading to long-term pain suppression.
