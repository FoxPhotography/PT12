2 Questions
Q1
Enumerate Heart Rate Training Zones with their target percentages.
▼
Heart Rate Zones

The standard heart rate zones used to structure cardiovascular training programs are based on percentages of Maximum Heart Rate (Max HR):

Zone Zone Name % of Max HR Primary Physiological Effect
1 Healthy Heart Zone (Warm up) 50% – 60% Active recovery, basic cardiac conditioning, warm-up/cool-down.
2 Weight Management Zone (Fat Burning) 60% – 70% Maximizes lipid utilization (fat metabolism), develops basic endurance.
3 Aerobic Zone (Cardiorespiratory Endurance) 70% – 80% Improves cardiovascular efficiency, aerobic capacity, glycogen sparing.
4 Anaerobic Threshold Zone (Performance Training) 80% – 90% Raises lactate threshold, builds high-intensity anaerobic endurance.
5 Red Line Zone (Maximum Effort) 90% – 100% Sprints, maximal neuromuscular power, rapid fatigue accumulation.
Q2
Differentiate between Physiological Responses and Adaptations to Exercise.
▼
Response vs Adaptation

Understanding the difference between acute physiological responses and chronic long-term adaptations to exercise training:

Feature Acute Responses Chronic Adaptations
Definition Sudden, temporary changes in homeostatic functions caused by physical exertion. Persistent, long-term modifications in structure or function following regular training.
Duration Disappear shortly after the exercise session is completed. Long-term/permanent; require several weeks/months of training to develop.
Dependency Depends directly on exercise duration, type, intensity, and frequency. Varies depending on intensity, volume, and the progressive overload principle.
Cardiac Examples
  • Heart Rate (HR) increases
  • Cardiac Output (Q) increases
  • Coronary circulation increases
  • Resting HR decreases (Bradycardia)
  • Stroke Volume (SV) increases at rest & work
  • Left ventricle chamber size/hypertrophy increases
Circulatory Examples
  • Body temperature increases
  • Vasodilation occurs in working muscles
  • Arteriovenous oxygen difference (a-vO₂) increases
  • Maintained elasticity of artery walls
  • Diminished fatty deposits in blood vessels
  • Blood volume and Hemoglobin count increases

Quick Summary — Key Concepts of Exercise Physiology
Anticipatory Response
Increased heart rate before exercise — caused by the release of epinephrine
Cardiovascular Drift
An increase in heart rate during steady exercise due to a reduction in stroke volume
Ejection Fraction
Percentage of blood ejected during each contraction (≈50% at rest, up to 100% in exercise)
Cardiac Output (Q) Formula
Q = SV × HR (resting is ~5 L/min)
ATP-CP System (Phosphagen)
Instantaneous energy, onset of exercise, exhausts in 10 seconds
Anaerobic Glycolysis (Lactic Acid)
Rapid supply, occurs in cytoplasm, lasts 35–60 seconds
Aerobic Glycolysis
Provides energy for prolonged activity (2–3 minutes or more, low-mod intensity)
Fat Yield (Aerobic Metabolism)
1 molecule of fat can yield up to 129 ATP (slower rate, requires high oxygen)
Crossover Concept Shift
Equilibrium intensity of lipids/CHO. Shifts to 60–70% VO₂max with training
Muscle Elasticity vs Extensibility
Elasticity: return to normal length | Extensibility: capacity to stretch
Type IIb Muscle Fibers
Fast-glycolytic, greatest anaerobic potential, fastest shortening velocity
Chronic Hypertrophy Cause
Increased contractile protein (increase in myofibrillar area & numbers)
Strength Training Capillarisation
No increase in capillaries; capillary density actually decreases
Endurance Training Blood Volume
Haemodilution increases resting blood volume by 8%
EPOC Early Phase
Falls very quickly within the first minute or two (fast component)
Second Wind Timing
Typically occurs after 2 to 4 minutes of exercise
VO₂max Increase Main Driver
Driven primarily by increase in stroke volume / cardiac output
Exercise Lung Volume Changes
Vital Capacity slightly decreases; TV increases, IRV/ERV decrease
Epinephrine Role
Increases cardiac pump volume & widens blood vessels (vasodilation)
Testosterone Secretion Profile
Rises for first 45–60 min of exercise and then continuously declines
Hypothyroidism Adaptations
Muscle fibers adapt and change type from fast-twitch to slow-twitch
EPOC / Endorphins
Released during pain/stress to block pain pathways and induce euphoria
Detraining (Reversibility) - 7 Weeks
27% loss of aerobic capacity, 30% SV drop, fitness declines at 1/3 rate of acquisition
Frostnip Definition
Mildest cold injury; characterized by reversible numbness upon rewarming
Salt Depletion Symptoms
Nausea, vomiting, muscle cramps, dizziness
