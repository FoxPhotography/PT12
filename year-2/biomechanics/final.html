Pathological Gait (الأسئلة الهامة المتوقعة)
6 أسئلة
Q1
Enumerate the Pathological Gait deviations caused by Weakness of Hip Extensors (Gluteus Maximus Gait).
▼
Normal Function

During initial contact to loading response, GRFV passes anterior to the hip, creating a flexion moment compensated by Gluteus Maximus.

Effect of Weakness

Inability to counteract the flexion moment, leading to a tendency for excessive hip flexion and anterior pelvic tilt.

Compensation (The Gait)

The patient will lean the trunk backward to shift the GRFV behind the axis of the hip joint to prevent the trunk from falling forward.

Long-term Effects
  • Excessive lumbar lordosis (Hyperlordosis).
  • Mechanical Low Back Pain (LBP).
Q2
Enumerate the Pathological Gait deviations and compensations caused by Weakness of Hip Abductors (Gluteus Medius / Trendelenburg Gait).
▼
Normal Function

During single limb support, GRFV lies medial to the hip, creating an adduction moment.

Effect of Weakness (Trendelenburg Sign)

Pelvis drops laterally toward the opposite side (the swing limb).

Possible Compensations
  1. Unilateral Weakness: The patient will lean the trunk laterally toward the weak/affected limb during its stance phase to shift GRFV through or lateral to the hip joint.
  2. Bilateral Weakness: Known as Waddling Gait or Duck Gait (leaning side-to-side).
Long-term Effects

Lateral spinal curve and functional scoliosis.

Q3
Write short notes on the Gait deviations caused by Weakness of Quadriceps (Knee Extensors).
▼
Effect of Weakness

Tendency for excessive knee flexion and knee instability at heel strike / loading response.

Possible Compensations
  1. Forward trunk lean to keep GRFV anterior to the knee joint line to lock it in extension passively.
  2. The patient compensates by pushing the femur posteriorly with his hand at mid stance to force knee extension.
  3. Increased activity of hip extensors and soleus muscle to pull the femur and tibia posteriorly.
Long-term Effects

Knock knee deformity and disabling fatigue.

Q4
Enumerate the Compensations for Weakness of Ankle Dorsiflexors (Tibialis Anterior / Pretibial Weakness).
▼
Effect of Weakness

Causes Foot Slap at loading response (loss of eccentric control) and Foot Drop or Toe Drag during the swing phase.

Possible Compensations (To clear the ground)
  1. High Steppage Gait: Increased/excessive hip and knee flexion during the swing phase (functional shortening of the affected limb).
  2. Hip Hiking on the affected side.
  3. Circumduction of the leg during swing.
  4. Shorter step length and flat-foot contact instead of heel strike.
Q5
Enumerate the effects of joint Contractures on Gait (Plantarflexion, Knee flexion, and Hip flexion contractures).
▼
1. Plantar Flexion Contracture
  • Blocks progression of the limb in the swing phase.
  • Inhibits floor/ground clearance.
2. Knee Flexion Contracture
  • Blocks progression during the stance phase.
  • Increases the activity/demand on the quadriceps to stabilize the knee.
3. Hip Flexion Contracture
  • Places the body's COG over the supporting foot, forcing the trunk to lean forward.
  • Introduces strain on the back muscles and hip extensors.
Q6
What are the Major Causes of Pathological Gait?
▼
Major Causes
  1. Deformity: (e.g., joint contractures, bony abnormalities).
  2. Impaired Motor Control or Spasticity: (Inhibits pre-swing preparation, causes sustained muscle action).
  3. Sensory Loss: Prevents the patient from knowing joint position (proprioceptive loss), causing slow walking.
  4. Pain (Antalgic Gait): Caused by excessive tissue tension or joint distension related to arthritis or trauma.
  5. Muscle Weakness: Insufficient muscle strength to meet the demands of walking (due to disuse atrophy or neurological impairment).
