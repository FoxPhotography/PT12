Essay Question Q103
Pilates, Active Exercises & Plyometrics
المطاليب الإنشائية والترتيب النموذجي:
1. Enumerate the Fundamentals of Pilates:
• Breathing (التنفس الصحيح)
• Centering (التركيز والبدء من المركز powerhouse)
• Concentration (التركيز الذهني الكامل)
• Control (التحكم في حركة العضلات والمفاصل)
• Precision (الدقة في الأداء والتكنيك)
• Flow / Fluidity (الانسيابية والحركة السلسة)
2. Enumerate the Goals of active exercises:
• Maintain or improve range of motion (ROM) & flexibility.
• Strengthen muscles (increase force output and hyper-trophy).
• Improve muscular endurance & aerobic capacity.
• Enhance balance, coordination, & stability (proprioception).
• Prevent joint stiffness, contractures, & muscle atrophy.
3. Enumerate the Levels of Plyometrics and discuss 2 of them:
أولاً: المستويات الثلاثة (3 Levels):
1. Easy / Low-intensity (e.g., squat jumps, ankle hops)
2. Medium / Moderate-intensity (e.g., tuck jumps, box jumps)
3. High / High-intensity (e.g., depth jumps, single-leg hops)

ثانياً: مناقشة مستويين بالتفصيل:
• Low-intensity (Easy): Focuses on sub-maximal stretch-shortening cycles, introducing bounding and ankle hops to build landing mechanics and joint resilience without extreme shear forces.
• High-intensity (Difficult): Incorporates ballistic movements like depth jumps (stepping off a box, landing, and instantly jumping up). It demands high eccentric control and extreme muscle-tendon stiffness to minimize transition phase (amortization).

المطاليب الإنشائية والترتيب النموذجي:
1. Enumerate the Basic Elements of Muscle Energy Technique (MET):
• Active patient contraction (concentric/isometric/eccentric).
• Controlled joint position and initial positioning at the barrier.
• Directed counterforce applied in a specific direction by the therapist.
• Controlled effort (usually light/moderate isometric force).
• Sustained contraction duration (typically 5 to 10 seconds).
• Complete muscle relaxation following contraction.
• Repositioning to the new restrictive barrier.
2. Enumerate the barriers included in the Barrier Concept:
• Physiological Barrier: The limit of active joint motion under voluntary muscle control.
• Anatomical Barrier: The absolute structural limit of passive joint motion, bounded by ligaments and bone-on-bone interfaces.
• Pathological Barrier (Restrictive Barrier): An abnormal limit within the physiological range, caused by muscle spasm, pain, fascial shortening, or joint effusion.
