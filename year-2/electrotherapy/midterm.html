Physical Laws
📐
State and explain the Inverse Square Law and Cosine Law in electrotherapy with their clinical significance.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Electromagnetic Spectrum & Physical Radiation Laws

1. Inverse Square Law:

Definition: The intensity of radiation (I) received by a surface is inversely proportional to the square of the distance (d) from the source: I ∝ 1 / d².

  • Clinical Significance: If the distance of an IR/UV lamp is doubled (e.g. from 50 cm to 100 cm), intensity drops to 1/4 (25%). If distance is halved, intensity increases 4 times (400%). Precise distance measurement is vital to avoid patient skin burns.

2. Cosine Law (Lambert's Law):

Definition: Maximum absorption of radiant energy occurs when the radiant beam strikes the skin at a right angle (90° / perpendicular). As the angle shifts from 90°, intensity decreases according to I = I₀ × cos θ.

  • Clinical Significance: The lamp head must always be aligned perpendicular to the target treatment area to ensure maximum therapeutic absorption and prevent wasteful reflection.
Physical Laws
📜
Explain Grotthus-Draper Law, Bunsen-Roscoe Law, and Arndt-Schultz Principle.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Electromagnetic Spectrum & Physical Radiation Laws
  • Grotthus-Draper Law: Only radiation that is absorbed by biological tissues can produce physical or chemical effects. Reflected or transmitted energy produces no biological response.
  • Bunsen-Roscoe Law of Reciprocity: The biological photochemical effect is determined by the total energy absorbed: Intensity × Exposure Time = Constant.
  • Arndt-Schultz Principle: Weak stimuli promote/excite physiological activity; moderate stimuli support and favor it; strong stimuli inhibit it; excessive stimuli abolish and destroy tissue.

UVR Physiology

Discuss the Physiological Effects of Ultraviolet Radiation on the Skin.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Ultraviolet Therapy (UVR)
  • 1. Delayed Erythema: Caused by UVB via gradual photochemical release and accumulation of Nitric Oxide and prostaglandins around dermal capillaries.
  • 2. Pigmentation (Tanning): Immediate tanning (UVA photo-oxidation of existing melanin) and delayed tanning (UVB stimulation of tyrosinase enzyme synthesizing new melanin in basal melanocytes).

  • 5. Immunosuppressive Effect: UVB destroys epidermal Langerhans cells (Birbeck granules) and stimulates suppressor T-cells, preventing autoimmune attacks in psoriasis.
  • 6. Bactericidal Effect: UV-C (260-280 nm) destroys bacterial DNA, used for sterilizing open infected wounds.

Performed on forearm/abdomen using a test sleeve with 5-6 cutout openings exposed at 15-30 second intervals. The MED is the exposure time producing a just-perceptible faint erythema at 6-8 hours that disappears within 24 hours without peeling.

US Parameters
📐
Define ERA, BNR, Acoustic Impedance, and Standing Waves in Ultrasound.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Therapeutic Ultrasound (US)
  • ERA (Effective Radiating Area): The actual surface area of the transducer crystal face that emits ultrasonic energy (always slightly smaller than the geometric soundhead face).
  • BNR (Beam Non-uniformity Ratio): Ratio of spatial peak intensity to spatial average intensity (I_peak / I_avg). Acceptable clinical range is 2:1 to 6:1. Values > 6:1 produce dangerous hot spots.
  • Acoustic Impedance (Z): The resistance of a tissue to sound wave transmission: Z = Tissue Density (ρ) × Velocity of Sound (c). Gel is mandatory to bridge the air-skin impedance mismatch.
  • Standing Waves: Created when reflected waves superimpose on incident waves at acoustic boundaries (e.g. bone periosteum). Prevented by continuously moving the transducer head at 2-4 cm/s.

▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Therapeutic Ultrasound (US)

Gate Control
🚪
Explain the Gate Control Theory of Pain (Melzack & Wall, 1965) with its clinical application in Physical Therapy.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Physiology of Pain in Physical Therapy

A. Mechanism of Gate Control:

  • Located in the Substantia Gelatinosa (SG - Lamina II) of the spinal dorsal horn.
  • Transmission projection cells (T-cells) transmit pain impulses up the spinothalamic tract to the brain.
  • Small nociceptive fibers (Aδ and C) carry noxious input and inhibit SG interneurons → Opens the Gate → T-cells fire actively → Pain is perceived.
  • Large non-nociceptive fibers (Aβ) carry touch/vibration and stimulate inhibitory SG interneurons → Closes the Gate → Presynaptic inhibition blocks Aδ/C neurotransmitter release → T-cell firing is suppressed → Pain is blocked.

B. Clinical Application:

Explains why rubbing a bruised knee, massage, vibration, and high-frequency TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) provide immediate pain relief.

Descending Pathway
🧬
Describe the Descending Pain Modulatory (Inhibitory) Pathway and the role of Endogenous Opioids.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Physiology of Pain in Physical Therapy

  • 2. Relay Nuclei: PAG sends excitatory projections to the Nucleus Raphe Magnus (NRM) in the rostral medulla.

Action Potential
⚡
Describe the Ionic Steps and Phases of a Nerve Action Potential.
▼ إظهار الإجابة النموذجية
📌 المحاضرة: Organization of Body Tissue & Histology / Nerve Bio-electricity
  • 1. Resting State: Resting Membrane Potential (RMP) is -70 mV (inside negative), maintained by high resting K⁺ permeability and the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump.
  • 2. Threshold: A depolarizing stimulus reaches -55 mV, triggering the opening of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.
  • 3. Depolarization Phase: Massive influx of Na⁺ rapidly drives the internal membrane potential up to +30 mV (All-or-None response).
  • 4. Repolarization Phase: Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels inactivate and close; voltage-gated K⁺ channels open slowly, causing rapid K⁺ efflux back toward resting potential.
  • 5. Hyperpolarization (Undershoot): Slower closure of K⁺ channels drives potential to -90 mV. This refractory state ensures unidirectional signal propagation.
  • 6. Return to Resting Potential: The active Na⁺/K⁺ pump (pumping 3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in) restores baseline ionic concentrations.
